# A computer-assisted unconditional `0.3827090879` bound for the union-closed sets conjecture

**Unsolved Labs Research Release R010 — 14 August 2026**

This note records a computer-assisted proof, with a reproducible interval-arithmetic checker, of the following statement.

> **Theorem.** Let \(\mathcal F\neq\{\varnothing\}\) be a finite nonempty union-closed family. Some element belongs to at least
> \[
> \frac{3827090879}{10000000000}\,|\mathcal F|
> \]
> members of \(\mathcal F\).

The argument uses Jingbo Liu's conditionally-i.i.d. protocol with \(f(t)=t(1-t)\), but it bypasses both numerical hypotheses in Section V of Liu's paper. The main new ingredient is a pointwise two-variable entropy inequality. One auxiliary inequality is proved analytically; the other is proved by elementary boundary estimates plus a directed-rounding interval certificate on a compact rectangle.

This is a **public research release with independent specialist review pending**. The original Boost.Interval checker has been reproduced with GCC and Clang and under address/undefined-behavior sanitizers. A separately written 256-bit MPFR interval checker, using a different transcendental backend and an independently generated subdivision, also certifies the numerical part. Its subdivision has additionally been frozen as a complete prefix-coded leaf certificate and replayed by a non-adaptive checker. External mathematical review and proof-assistant formalization remain desirable.

## 1. Notation

Let

\[
h(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\]

where the logarithm is natural. Changing the logarithm base multiplies every entropy term by the same positive constant and does not affect any inequality below.

Set

\[
J(x,y)=xy\bigl(1+(1-x)(1-y)\bigr),
\qquad
j(x)=J(x,x)=x^2\bigl(1+(1-x)^2\bigr).
\]

Fix the exact constants

\[
\alpha=\frac{11249343}{12500000},\qquad
\beta=\frac{1250657}{12500000},\qquad
c=\frac{3827090879}{10000000000},\qquad
m=1-c=\frac{6172909121}{10000000000},
\]

and

\[
\kappa=\frac1{2m}=\frac{5000000000}{6172909121}.
\]

## 2. The two pointwise inequalities

The proof uses the following two statements.

### Lemma 1: the conditionally-i.i.d. kernel lower bound

For all \(x,y\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
 h(J(x,y))\ge \sqrt{h(j(x))h(j(y))}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2: the main entropy inequality

For all \(x,y\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{11249343}{12500000}h(xy)
+\frac{1250657}{12500000}\sqrt{h(j(x))h(j(y))}
\ge
\frac{5000000000}{6172909121}\bigl(xh(y)+yh(x)\bigr).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Lemma 1 is proved analytically in Section 4. Lemma 2 is proved in Sections 5–7, with the compact central region certified by `union_closed_sharp_verifier.cpp`.

## 3. Direct deduction of the union-closed bound

This section gives the coordinatewise argument explicitly, including the
strictness needed at the endpoint.

Let \(\mathcal F\neq\{\varnothing\}\) be union-closed, and let \(Z\) be
uniform on the indicator vectors of the members of \(\mathcal F\).  The case
\(|\mathcal F|=1\) is immediate, so assume \(|\mathcal F|>1\).  For a prefix
\(z^{i-1}\), write

\[
p_i(z^{i-1})=\Pr[Z_i=1\mid Z^{i-1}=z^{i-1}].
\]

Use two sequential couplings of copies of \(Z\).

* Under protocol \(\Pi^{(0)}\), the two coordinate bits are independent
  Bernoulli variables with the prescribed conditional means.
* Under protocol \(\Pi^{(1)}\), use Liu's conditionally-i.i.d. protocol from
  Example 5 with \(f(r)=r(1-r)\).

Let \((A^{(k)},B^{(k)})\) be the pair generated by protocol \(k\), and put
\(W^{(k)}=A^{(k)}\vee B^{(k)}\).  Each marginal \(A^{(k)}\) and \(B^{(k)}\)
has the law of \(Z\).

Fix a coordinate \(i\).  If

\[
S=p_i(A^{(k),i-1}),\qquad T=p_i(B^{(k),i-1}),
\]

then \(S\) and \(T\) have the same marginal law, independent of \(k\).  Let
\(P_i\) denote the law of \(X=1-S\), and set

\[
M_i=\mathbb E X=1-\Pr[Z_i=1],
\qquad
H_i=\mathbb E h(X)=H(Z_i\mid Z^{i-1}).
\]

For the independent protocol, the transformed variables \(X=1-S\) and
\(Y=1-T\) are independent with law \(P_i\), and the conditional probability
that the OR bit is zero is \(XY\).  Integrating (2.2) against \(P_i\times P_i\)
gives

\[
\alpha\,\mathbb E h(XY)
+\beta\left(\mathbb E\sqrt{h(j(X))}\right)^2
\ge \frac{M_i}{m}H_i.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For the conditionally-i.i.d. protocol, Liu's construction implies that the two
transformed conditional probabilities \(X,Y\) are i.i.d. conditional on an
auxiliary variable \(U\).  Example 5 gives

\[
\Pr[A_i^{(1)}\vee B_i^{(1)}=0\mid A^{(1),i-1},B^{(1),i-1}]
=J(X,Y).
\]

Writing \(s(x)=\sqrt{h(j(x))}\), Lemma 1, conditional independence, and Jensen
give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E h(J(X,Y))
&\ge \mathbb E[s(X)s(Y)]\\
&=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbb E(s(X)\mid U)^2\right]\\
&\ge \left(\mathbb E s(X)\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Combining (3.1) and (3.2), the weighted coordinate contribution of the two
protocols satisfies

\[
\alpha\,\mathbb E h(XY)
+\beta\,\mathbb E h(J(X,Y))
\ge \frac{M_i}{m}H_i.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For either protocol, the chain rule and the fact that
\(W^{(k),i-1}\) is a function of \((A^{(k),i-1},B^{(k),i-1})\) yield

\[
H(W^{(k)})
\ge \sum_i H(W_i^{(k)}\mid A^{(k),i-1},B^{(k),i-1}).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Assume, toward a contradiction, that every element occurs in strictly fewer
than \(c|\mathcal F|\) members of \(\mathcal F\).  Then \(M_i>m=1-c\) for
every coordinate.  Summing (3.3) and using (3.4) gives

\[
\alpha H(W^{(0)})+\beta H(W^{(1)})
\ge \sum_i \frac{M_i}{m}H_i
> \sum_i H_i
= H(Z)=\log|\mathcal F|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The strict inequality holds because \(H(Z)>0\), so at least one \(H_i\) is
positive.  On the other hand, union closure implies that both \(W^{(0)}\) and
\(W^{(1)}\) are supported on \(\mathcal F\), hence each has entropy at most
\(\log|\mathcal F|\).  Their weighted average cannot exceed
\(\log|\mathcal F|\), contradicting (3.5).  This proves the theorem.

## 4. Analytic proof of Lemma 1

The boundary cases are immediate by continuity, so assume \(0<x\le y<1\). Define

\[
\ell(t)=\log h(t),
\qquad
R(x,y)=2\ell(J(x,y))-\ell(j(x))-\ell(j(y)).
\]

Since \(h''<0\) and \(h>0\),

\[
\ell''=\frac{h''}{h}-\left(\frac{h'}h\right)^2<0,
\]

so \(\ell\) is strictly concave. Also \(R(x,x)=0\). It is enough to prove \(R_y(x,y)\ge0\).

We use

\[
J(x,y)\le j(y),\qquad 2J_y(x,y)\le j'(y),\qquad x\le y.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Both follow by direct expansion.

### 4.1. The region \(j(y)\ge1/2\)

If \(J(x,y)\le1/2\le j(y)\), then \(\ell'(J)\ge0\) and \(\ell'(j(y))\le0\), so

\[
R_y=2\ell'(J)J_y-\ell'(j(y))j'(y)\ge0.
\]

If \(1/2\le J(x,y)\le j(y)\), concavity gives

\[
\ell'(J(x,y))\ge\ell'(j(y)),
\]

and both quantities are nonpositive. Together with (4.1), this again implies \(R_y\ge0\).

### 4.2. The region \(j(y)\le1/2\)

Let \(G(x,y)=\ell(J(x,y))\). Since

\[
\frac d{dy}\ell(j(y))=2G_y(y,y),
\]

we have

\[
R_y(x,y)=2\bigl(G_y(x,y)-G_y(y,y)\bigr).
\]

Thus it suffices to prove \(G_{xy}\le0\) in this region.
